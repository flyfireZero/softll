\section{Large-Scale Benchmarks (Experiments 6 to 10)}
After tuning the model on WikiText-2, we ran five larger experiments on TinyStories. Here we looked at how well the model scales with more data, whether it resists distractor noise in the prompt, how it handles multi-step logic, and how it performs on bidirectional masked language modeling.

\subsection{Exp 6: Big Dataset Scaling on TinyStories}
We trained both models on TinyStories for 500 steps (processing 1.02 million tokens). In this first run, Soft L-Topology achieved slightly better loss (3.7098 vs. 3.7443) and lower perplexity (40.85 vs. 42.28) than the baseline Transformer. Over this run, \textbf{60.25\% of all attention connections dropped to exact zero}. Generating text with our continuous lattice KV-cache ran at 172.4 tokens/second on our GTX 1650 GPU.

In this 6.14M-token benchmark (Table \ref{tab:exp9_results}, Figure \ref{fig:exp9_convergence}), the baseline Transformer achieved lower validation loss (3.4866 vs. 3.7455) and lower perplexity (32.67 vs. 42.33). Because softmax distributes nonzero probabilities over all tokens, gradient descent encounters a smoother surface when minimizing cross-entropy log-loss. In contrast, Soft L-Topology applies a strict zero cutoff whenever attribute overlap drops below threshold $\tau$.

Even with this steeper optimization landscape, the lattice model remained stable and maintained \textbf{55.06\% exact-zero attention weights} throughout training. The model also generated text with broader lexical variety: Distinct-1 and Distinct-2 scores rose by 2.57\%, while Distinct-3 increased by \textbf{4.19\%}. During generation, standard attention occasionally fell into repetitive loops (such as repeating $\langle\text{endoftext}\rangle$), whereas Soft L-Topology bypassed these traps by cutting connections to stale past tokens.

\subsection{Exp 10: Bidirectional Encoder MLM Edge}
In bidirectional Masked Language Modeling (Figure \ref{fig:exp10_encoder}), Soft L-Topology matched and slightly outperformed BERT attention on held-out loss (6.2041 vs. 6.2422). Under a 49-token distractor flood, the baseline lost 75.76\% of its attention mass to irrelevant context, whereas our router clipped 50.00\% of distractor connections to exact zero ($0.0000$), holding leakage down to 38.28\% (a 37.48 percentage point reduction).

\section{Critical Discussion and Explainability}

\subsection{Why the Perplexity Gap Exists}
Cross-entropy loss $\mathcal{L} = -\sum \log P(w)$ heavily penalizes small probabilities because $-\log(p)$ approaches infinity as $p \to 0$. Softmax guards against this penalty by diffusing small probability mass across the entire vocabulary, producing an easy, well-conditioned gradient path. Soft L-Topology intentionally severs weak links to exact zero. While this sharp cutoff prevents distractor leakage and guarantees 55\% sparsity, it penalizes cross-entropy when the model assigns low probability to true tokens.

\subsection{Explainable AI (XAI) and Hardware Profiling}
Having deterministic zeros also sharpens interpretability: unrouted tokens evaluate to exactly $0.0000$, passing zero activation and zero gradient, which removes the dense background attention haze common to softmax. On hardware, our PyTorch prototype relies on 4D tensor broadcasts that achieve 62\% of cuBLAS GEMM throughput. A dedicated fused CUDA kernel (FlashLattice) evaluating the threshold directly in GPU SRAM could skip zero writes and turn this 55\% mathematical sparsity into an estimated 1.5$\times$ to 2$\times$ wall-clock speedup.
