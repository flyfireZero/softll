\section{Large-Scale Benchmarks (Experiments 6 to 10)}
Following initial validation on WikiText-2, we conducted five benchmark experiments on TinyStories to evaluate scaling behavior, resilience to context distractors, logical transitivity, open-ended generation quality, and masked language modeling.

\subsection{Exp 6: Big Dataset Scaling on TinyStories}
We trained both architectures for 500 steps on TinyStories (1.02M tokens). In this initial scaling run, Soft L-Topology achieved slightly lower training loss (3.7098 vs. 3.7443) and perplexity (40.85 vs. 42.28) than the baseline Transformer, while operating with \textbf{60.25\% of all token connections set to exact zero}. Autoregressive generation with continuous lattice KV-caching reached 172.4 tokens/second on the GTX 1650.

\subsection{Exp 8: Multi-Step Deductive Entailment Benchmark}
We tested multi-step Modus Ponens reasoning ($A \to B \to C$) to evaluate logical transitivity. Soft L-Topology achieved \textbf{40.00\% accuracy} with a positive confidence margin (+0.00021), compared to 20.00\% for the standard Transformer. The continuous subsethood formulation encourages tokens to preserve transitive entailment relationships better than standard dot-product attention.

\subsection{Exp 10: Bidirectional Encoder MLM Edge}
In bidirectional Masked Language Modeling (Figure \ref{fig:exp10_encoder}), Soft L-Topology achieved a slightly lower validation loss than standard BERT-style attention (6.2041 vs. 6.2422). When tested with 49 injected distractor tokens, the baseline lost 75.76\% of its attention to distractors. In comparison, Soft L-Topology pruned 50.00\% of distractor weights to exact zero, reducing total distractor leakage to 38.28\% (a 37.48 percentage point reduction).

\section{Critical Discussion and Explainability}

\subsection{Why the Perplexity Gap Exists}
Cross-entropy loss $\mathcal{L} = -\sum \log P(w)$ heavily penalizes small probabilities assigned to target words ($-\log(p) \to \infty$ as $p \to 0$). Because softmax distributes continuous probability mass across all context tokens, it provides a smooth loss surface that assists gradient descent in finding good likelihoods. In contrast, Soft L-Topology enforces an exact threshold: when attribute overlap drops below $\tau$, weights drop strictly to zero. While this cutoff prevents distractor leakage and yields 55\% routing sparsity, it makes next-token probability optimization more demanding, creating the observed perplexity gap.

\subsection{Explainable AI (XAI) and Hardware Profiling}
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.5pt, topsep=1.0pt, parsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Explainability:} Standard attention heatmaps contain background noise across all tokens, obscuring which inputs drove a prediction. In Soft L-Topology, unrouted tokens evaluate strictly to zero ($0.0000$), guaranteeing zero activation and zero gradient flow.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Throughput:} In standard PyTorch, Soft L-Topology reaches about 62\% of cuBLAS GEMM throughput due to intermediate 4D operations. A fused GPU kernel (FlashLattice) evaluating the nilpotent threshold in SRAM before global memory writes could turn this sparsity into a 1.5$\times$ to 2$\times$ wall-clock speedup.
\end{itemize}
