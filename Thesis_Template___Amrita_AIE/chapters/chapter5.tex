\section{Large-Scale Benchmarks (Experiments 6 to 10)}
Having calibrated the architecture on WikiText-2, we executed five large-scale empirical benchmarks on TinyStories to evaluate scaling, distractor immunity, logical transitivity, autoregressive narrative generation, and bidirectional representation learning.

\subsection{Exp 6: Big Dataset Scaling on TinyStories}
Both models were trained for 500 steps on TinyStories (1.02M tokens processed). In this regime, Soft L-Topology achieved both lower training loss (3.7098 vs. 3.7443) and lower perplexity (40.85 vs. 42.28) than the standard Transformer, while operating with \textbf{60.25\% of all token connections severed to exact zero}. Continuous lattice KV-cached inference achieved 172.4 tokens/second on consumer GPU hardware.

\section{Critical Discussion and Explainability}

\subsection{Why the Perplexity Gap Exists}
To explain this honestly: the cross-entropy objective $\mathcal{L} = -\sum \log P(w)$ heavily penalizes assigning near-zero probability to plausible vocabulary tokens ($-\log(p) \to \infty$ as $p \to 0$). Softmax acts like a \textit{soft sponge} that absorbs fractional signal from every token in context, distributing smooth non-zero probability across thousands of tail tokens. Soft L-Topology acts like a \textit{sharp logical filter}: when semantic overlap falls below the nilpotent threshold, connections drop to absolute zero. Consequently, softmax has an inductive bias tailored for maximizing smooth cross-entropy likelihood, whereas Soft L-Topology is tailored for crisp information containment, distractor isolation, and exact sparsity.

\subsection{Explainable AI (XAI) and Hardware Profiling}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Faithful Explainability:} In standard attention heatmaps, diffuse background noise makes it impossible to distinguish true semantic relevance from softmax artifacts. In Soft L-Topology, unrouted tokens evaluate strictly to 0.0000, guaranteeing zero activation and zero gradient flow.
    \item \textbf{Hardware Throughput Profile:} In pure PyTorch without custom CUDA kernels, Soft L-Topology executes at $\approx 62\%$ the throughput of cuBLAS GEMM matrix multiplications due to element-wise 4D tensor broadcasting. Developing a fused GPU kernel (FlashLattice) that evaluates the nilpotent threshold in on-chip SRAM before writing to HBM represents the natural path to convert theoretical sparsity into a 1.5$\times$ to 2$\times$ wall-clock speedup.
\end{itemize}
